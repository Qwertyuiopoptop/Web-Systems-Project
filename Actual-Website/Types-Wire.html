Types of Wire

Different wires can contain different recyclable metals.

Standard Wires:

Insulated copper #2 - not worth stripping. When you look at the wire it is made up of skinny strands. To be worth stripping it needs to be the diameter of a pencil led and solid - not strands wound together. If it is the diameter of pencil led and stripped it can be considered bare bright copper, which prices more per pound.

Copper Wire:

Copper from various electronics. It is wound around plastic and sometimes magnets.
I unwind the copper so that I get a better price per pound. This is copper #2 grade. Often there is glue that you have to remove (break, chip off, smash - whatever works - it can get messy). I usually save this for rainy days or Packer Games.

Learn more about copper

Ribbon Wires:

This is ribbon wire - insulated copper #3 - not worth stripping. Found in computers, monitors and TVs - almost everything. Just rip it out of whatever it is connected to.

Aluminum and Copper Wiring:

Inside of a power supply. I remove the wires (red, yellow, black), and the aluminum. Then remove any copper and set aside to unwind later. The rest of the circuit board goes into the sheet iron bin. Sheet iron is the lowest priced recycled metal (price is per ton).
